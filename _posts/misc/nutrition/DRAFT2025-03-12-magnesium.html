--- layout: post title: "Mag-nificent: The Mighty Mineral for Muscles and Mind" date: 2025-03-12 00:00:00 -05000 categories: - Misc. - Nutrition permalink: /misc/magnesium image: /assets/Misc/Nutrition/Magnesium/Magnesium.png tags: - nutrient - vitamin - micronutrient - magnesium - sleep - muscle --- Introduction
 Magnesium is one of the most important micronutrients, playing a supportive role in muscle recovery, sleep, and stress reduction, just to name a few. Outside of iron, calcium, and potassium, no other mineral is required to be present on a nutrition label. Which is a shame, because most people should be paying more attention to their magnesium intake.


 Along with sodium and potassium, magnesium is a critical electrolyte for your body. Often lost during sweating, magnesium plays a vital role in forming the structures of proteins, mitochondria, and bones in your body.


High Magnesium Foods
  • Meat
  • Fish
  • Beans
  • Vegatables
  • Fruit
  • Nuts and Seeds
  • Grains
  • Chocolate


Why Do You Need Magnesium?
 It's estimated that 75% of American adults are magnesium deficient. This is largely in part due to our ultra-processed diet, which is entirely void of most micronutrients, magnesium included. Most people don't realize they're deficient in this crucial mineral.


 Optimal magnesium consumption is linked with better sleep, lower risk of depression and anxiety, protection against heart disease and Type 2 Diabetes, lower risk of cancer, lower blood pressure and cortisol, and improved mood. Quite literally, a magnesium supplement is a "chill pill"


 Consuming optimal levels of magnesium, either through food or supplementation, is vital to your health. Magnesium plays a role in protein synthesis, creation of DNA and RNA, muscle recovery and relaxation, and preserving nerve and brain cells.


Benefits of Magnesium
  • Lower blood pressure
  • Lower blood sugar
  • Improved mood
  • Combats depression and anxiety
  • Better sleep
  • Enhanced ATP production
  • Improved immune system
  • Improved thyroid health
  • Lower risk of diabetes
  • Lower risk of cancer
  • Improved cardiovascular health
  • Quicker muscle recovery
  • Preventing fatigue and light-headedness
  • Reducing brain fog
  • Improved insulin sensitivity
  • Combats metabolic syndrome
  • Prevents muscle cramps
  • Lower cortisol


Symptoms of Low Magnesium Intake (Hypomagnesemia)
  • Nausea
  • Fatigue
  • Appetite loss
  • Anxiety
  • Depression
  • Migraines
  • Muscle cramps
  • High blood pressure
  • Type 2 Diabetes
  • Insulin Resistance
  • Metabolic Syndrome
  • Heart disease
  • Chronic Inflammation
  • Brain fog
  • Osteoporosis
  • Mood swings
  • Elevated cortisol
  • Elevated glucose, insulin, and A1C


Daily Consumption
  • Men: 400 mg
  • Women: 310 mg
  • Note: Absorption rate of magnesium is about 30-40%, and can be improved with sufficient protein and vitamin D


Optimal Test Range
  • 5.0 - 6.4 mg/dL
