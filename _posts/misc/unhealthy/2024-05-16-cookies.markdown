Description: Here's a standard chocolate chip cookie recipe. I usually only make and post healthy recipes, but it's okay to enjoy the classic treat from time to time. Chocolate chip cookies are usually what I make to bring for an occasion; that way I can have 1 or 2 every so often, and I can leave someone else with the rest.  I made these to celebrate Vic getting into UF for her Master's Degree.  Brownies are the other classic dessert, so check out my <a href='/recipes/brownies'>Classic Fudgy Brownies</a>, or check out any of my other <a href='/cookies'>healthy cookie recipes</a>!
Instructions:
- Preheat your oven to 350F, and line 2 large cookie sheets with parchment paper

- Mix together sugar, honey, and (melted) butter with a hand mixer until fluffy. Beat in egg and mix in vanilla, salt, and baking soda. Fully combine, then mix in the flour. Fold in the chocolate chips

- Chill the dough in the fridge (at least 30 minutes) if using melted butter. This step is not necessary if butter is softened

- Scoop the cookies, roll into balls, and place on the pan.  These will flatten and spread as they bake, so leave plenty of room in between.  Don't do more than 12 on a standard size cookie sheet

- Bake for about 8-9 min at 350F, or until lightly golden brown on the bottom and the sides. Allow them to cool on the pan for a few minutes, until they can be safely scraped off with a spatula without breaking.  Transfer to a wire rack to cool

- I typically use vanilla extract, but almond extract is also great in these cookies.  Or, consider using mint extract for mint chocolate chip cookies.  Mint is much stronger though; don't use any more than 1/4 tsp

- If you're like me and typically only have <a href='/misc/grains#whole-wheat'>whole wheat</a> flour handy (instead of <a href='/misc/grains#white-wheat'>white flour</a>), use 215 g instead of 240 g (just under 2 cups), since <a href='/misc/grains#whole-wheat'>whole wheat</a> flour absorbs more water than all purpose

- If you want to make the cookies <a href='/misc/celiac'>gluten free</a>, just replace the <a href='/misc/grains#white-flour'>all purpose flour</a> (2 cups, 240 g) with 1.5 cups (180 g) <a href='/misc/grains#oats'>oat</a>, <a href='/misc/nuts#almonds'>almond</a>, or <a href='/misc/grains#millet'>millet</a> flour, and 1/2 cup (60 g) cornstarch

- You can make these into <a href='/misc/nuts#peanuts'>peanut</a> butter chocolate chip cookies as well.  Just add 1/2 cup (128 g) of melted peanut butter to the cookie dough along with the sugar and butter.  Additionally, cut the flour in half, from 2 cups (240 g) to 1 cup (120 g).  Bake as normal for about 9 minutes

- Cutting the flour in half also applies to <a href='/misc/celiac'>gluten free</a> and <a href='/misc/grains#whole-wheat'>whole wheat</a> peanut butter cookies as well.  For GF, use 3/4 cup (90 g) oat, almond, or <a href='/misc/grains#millet'>millet</a> flour, and 1/4 cup (30 g) of cornstarch in place of the <a href='/misc/grains#white-flour'>all purpose flour</a> (1 cup, 120 g).  For <a href='/misc/grains#whole-wheat'>whole wheat</a>, use just under 1 cup (108 g) <a href='/misc/grains#whole-wheat'>whole wheat</a> flour instead of the all purpose

- If you want to make these healthier, replace the <a href='/misc/dairy#butter'>butter</a>, <a href='/misc/hidden-sugar'>sugar</a>, and <a href='/misc/grains#white-wheat'>white flour</a> with <a href='/misc/fruit#coconut'>coconut</a> oil, granulated monk fruit, and <a href='/misc/grains#oats'>oat</a> flour.  Use 1/2 cup (108 g) melted coconut oil, 1 cup (200 g) granulated monk fruit, and 2.33 cup (210 g) oat flour
