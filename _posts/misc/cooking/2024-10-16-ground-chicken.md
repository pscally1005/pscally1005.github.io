<u><b><font size="+2">Description</font></b></u><br>
&emsp;Ground chicken is a great alternative to <a href='/misc/meat#ground-beef'>ground beef</a> or <a href='/misc/meat#ground-turkey'>ground turkey</a> that I like to use on occasion.  I've used it in recipes before like my <a href='/recipes/chicken-nuggets'>Freezer Chicken Nuggets</a>, <a href='/recipes/low-fodmap-tacos'>Low FODMAP Chicken Tacos</a>, and <a href='/recipes/lemon-feta-bowl'>Lemon Feta Chicken Bowls</a>.  You can really use ground chicken in place of any type of <a href='/recipes/ground-meat'>ground meat</a> for slightly different flavor and nutrition.

&emsp;Chicken breasts are about 99% lean, and chicken <a href='/misc/meat#chicken-thighs'>thighs</a> are about 93% lean.  Compared to <a href='/misc/meat#ground-beef'>beef</a> and turkey (both 93%), chicken is a leaner option.  But ground chicken is always much more expensive.  I can get 93/7 <a href='/misc/meat#ground-beef'>ground beef</a> for about $4.50 per pound and 93/7 <a href='/misc/meat#ground-turkey'>ground turkey</a> for about $4.00 per pound.  But ground chicken <a href='/misc/meat#chicken-thighs'>thighs</a> and ground chicken breasts are normally about $4.50 per pound and $5.50 per pound, respectively.

&emsp;Today, I wanted to try saving some money and making my own ground chicken.  Since I can get boneless skinless chicken breasts for about $2.50 per pound (and <a href='/misc/meat#chicken-thighs'>thighs</a> for about $3.00 per pound), I could save over half by just grinding it myself.  And it's very simple to do; let me show you.

<u><b><font size="+2">Instructions</font></b></u><br>
<ol>
    <li>
        Trim the fat off your chicken, and cut into 1" cubes.  Transfer to a cookie sheet.  Either chicken breasts or <a href='/misc/meat#chicken-thighs'>thighs</a> will work here, but today I'm going with breasts.
        <br><br>
        <center><img src='/assets/Misc/Cooking/Ground Chicken/whole-breasts.jpg' alt="" class="half-page"><img src='/assets/Misc/Cooking/Ground Chicken/cubed.jpg' alt="" class="half-page"></center>
    </li><br>
    <li>
        Freeze the food processor blade and the cookie sheet (uncovered) for 30 minutes.
    </li><br>
    <li>
        Pulse in a food processor (about 10 times of 1 second each) until you have a ground chicken consistency, about the size of a grain of <a href='/misc/grains#brown-rice'>rice</a>.  Transfer to a bowl.  You may need to blend in batches depending on how much chicken you have; I did 3 for these 4 large breasts.
        <br><br>
        <center><img src='/assets/Misc/Cooking/Ground Chicken/ground.jpg' alt="" class="instruction-image"></center>
    </li><br>
    <li>
        At this point you have raw ground chicken!  Use any way you'd normally use any ground meat.  Here I'm just sauteing it in a pan until fully cooked, and adding in a can of fava beans, some <a href='/recipes/bbq-sauce'>Unsweetened BBQ Sauce</a>, and spices (chili powder, garlic powder, paprika, onion powder, black pepper, and salt)
        <br><br>

    </li>
</ol>
