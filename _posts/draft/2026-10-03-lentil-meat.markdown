Description: I've been seeing a lot of recipes pop up lately for adding lentils to your meat, helping to stretch them out and feed more people, helping to save money. Truly a recession indicator. But it's a great idea, allowing you to add roughly 25% more servings, as well as some fiber, protein, iron, and folate. With beef prices these days, it's damn near necessary to stretch that out as much as possible. So today, I'm making a taco meat by mixing ground beef with cooked lentils and taco seasoning.
Instructions:
- Add lentils, water, and salt to a small pot, covered. Bring to boil over high heat, then reduce heat to low. Simmer with the cover on for about 20 minutes, or until the lentils are tender.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Ground Meat/Lentil meat/lentils.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

  Meanwhile, heat a large pan over medium heat with olive oil. Cut an onion into a small dice. When the oil is hot, add in the onion, and cook until it starts to turn translucent, about 5 minutes.<br><br>

  Add in the meat, and press it down as much as possible to make sure as much of meat is touching the pan. Let it sear for about 5 minutes without touching it to develop good browning, until fragrant.<br><br>

  Mash with a wooden spoon, and continue cooking until no longer pink.<br><br>

  Make a hole in the center, and add the garlic to the pan. Cook for about 30 seconds, until fragrant. Add in the lime juice, and mix the lime and garlic into the meat.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Ground Meat/Lentil meat/meat.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Meanwhile, mix together all the spices in a small bowl.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Ground Meat/Lentil meat/spices.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- When the lentils are done cooking and the meat is no longer pink, add all your lentils and spice mix into the meat. Mix together, and cook for an additional 2 minutes, or until you can smell the spices. Season with salt or lime juice to taste, and serve.

Notes:
- Any lentils will work, like brown lentils, green lentils, or red lentils.

- Any ground meat will work, like ground beef, ground turkey, ground chicken, or ground pork.

- 1/2 cup (96 g) of raw lentils yields about 1 1/3 cup (260 g) of cooked lentils.

- Typically, 1 lb (454 g) of raw meat serves 4 people. By adding the lentils here, we can increase the number of servings to 5 people per pound of meat, or an increase of 25%.

- You can scale this recipe up or down to add the amount of lentils you need to meat to be able to feed more people.

- Below are the nutrition facts assuming 1 lb (454 g) 93% ground beef with all the spices, serving 5 people, both with and without the lentils. 5 servings is too small for just 1 lb (454 g) of meat, but the addition of the lentils make it a bigger serving size for cheaper. Moreover, the lentils add fiber, folate, and plant-based protein & iron.
