Introduction
You may take a vacation, but your health never does. It's absolutely okay to relax, enjoy life, and try new and exciting foods. But it's also possible to enjoy yourself while still keeping some of your healthy habits intact. The goal is to make sure you're still working some exercise and healthy eating into your day.

A simple approach is to roughly follow the 90% rule. If you're eating well and exercising most of the time, you can leave some room for treats, restaurant meals, and an occasional day off.

The 90% rule isn't meant to be a literal calculation. It's just a reminder that you don't need to be perfect all the time. Vacation doesn't have to be perfect to be healthy.

The Two Extremes
The two extremes of someone on vacation are pretty easy to imagine. Person A is someone who eats and drinks everything. They "don't exercise on vacation", and eat indulgent food for breakfast, lunch, and dinner, plus 1-3 desserts a day. They have a good time, sure, but they come back feeling sluggish and potentially several pounds heavier.

On the other hand, person B panics as soon as a vacation is planned. They stress obsessively about what they're going to eat and where they're going to work out. They're worried the whole time about what they're eating, and they have a hard time enjoying themselves. They may avoid the sluggishness, but they've spent the entire vacation worrying about food and exercise.

Don't be person A. And don't be person B. Instead, be person C. The one who works out in the morning. The one who makes sure to get plenty of protein, vegetables, and fruit throughout the day. But the one who isn't afraid to eat a slice of cake.

So I just got back from a cruise, the inspiration for this blog post. There's tons of indulgent food available at all times, but also some healthy food options and a gym on standby.

I'm going to break down examples of breakfast, lunch, dinner, and snacks/desserts for both maintaining your health goals and going completely balls-to-the-wall, "I don't care," Standard American. I'm also going to show you how to work in exercise.

The cottage cheese is my main protein source, with the almonds and walnuts adding healthy fats. Sugar free syrup is optional, but adds a touch of sweetness.

And that's just for the days I was on the boat. When I'm at an airport, an Airbnb, or while traveling elsewhere, my go-to breakfast is normally the following:

  • Protein shake
  • Nuts
  • Carrots
  • Apple

Before I go on a trip, I prepare multiple single-serving bags of my protein shake mix. This is my To Go Protein Shake, which is a mix of protein powder, milk powder, cocoa powder, cinnamon, and pure granulated monk fruit in a Ziploc bag. I also added psyllium husk for fiber.

I'll just travel with a shaker bottle, dump in the bag of powder, and all I need to do is add water. It's cheap, simple, and a tasty way to get protein in on the go.

The other food I prep is nuts. I'll prepare multiple single-serving bags of nuts of 1 oz (28 g) each. This time it was pistachios. This provides my healthy fats.

The last 2 components are baby carrots for some vegetables and fiber, and an apple for some fruit and carbs.

Other (Desserts, Drinks, etc.)
Things tend to go haywire on a vacation. Going out to ice cream after lunch. Ordering a slice of cake at dinner.

And there's the alcohol. Alcohol is also calorie-dense at 7 calories per gram, and many alcoholic drinks contain substantial amounts of added sugar. If you drink, enjoy it, but remember that those calories can add up quickly.

Let's assume the following vacationer has these foods and drinks outside of their usual three meals:

  • Bag of Doritos
  • Vanilla ice cream with hot fudge
  • 2 chocolate chip cookies
  • Large slice of chocolate cake
  • 2 mixed drinks

If the calories weren't already off the charts with breakfast, lunch, and dinner, then well they are now. I can't stress this enough, please enjoy yourself, but treat your body well while you're treating yourself.

Now, onto the "healthier" vacationer. I'm putting healthy in quotes. Why's that?

Do you remember the 90% rule? Eat healthy and exercise 90% of the time, so you have that 10% wiggle room to "enjoy life", relax, and treat yourself? Yeah, that's gonna come in handy right about now.

Again, I'll be assuming the cruise. As you can tell, I've been carefully building very nutritious breakfasts, lunches, and dinners. And this is why, because here's my 10%.

Sampling different options is a great way to enjoy new or exciting flavors for foods you wouldn't necessarily want a full portion of because of one reason or another. Whether it's full of added sugar, has refined flour or bread, is oily or greasy, or is just calorie dense, sampling is the way to go. That works especially well at buffets and cruises because you can have a bite of something interesting without turning every interesting food into an entire meal.

Exercise
There's a common misconception that "I don't need to exercise on vacation". I'm here to tell you that you don't have to stop exercising just because you're on vacation. You don't necessarily need to exercise more on vacation, but there's also no reason you have to stop exercising entirely.

On a vacation, you're not eating home cooked food. This generally means bigger portion sizes, especially with buffets, as well as more fried food and otherwise added oils, increased bread consumption, and added sugar on top of added sugar.

You're likely eating more food at once, more often, and worse quality food. Again, please enjoy your once in a while vacation, but while you may take time off work, your health does not.

Rather than abandoning exercise entirely, try to keep some movement in your routine. A morning workout can be a great way to start the day such as cardio or weightlifting. Walking around and exploring your destination throughout the day can add plenty of activity without feeling like exercise.

For me, this meant getting to the gym right when I roll out of bed, and hopping on the exercise bike for an hour before showering and going to breakfast.

I may also throw in a couple sets of curls while I'm there, or get some quick stretching done. Perfect, it's not even 8am, and I already got my main workout in.

We did a bunch of walking around the island, as well as some treadmill walking too. Especially with a cruise, you could be lying around by the pool or in your room for a while.

If I biked before breakfast, then I would squeeze in a 30 minute, 2 mile walk, each after lunch and dinner. Post-meal walks can help with digestion and post-meal blood sugar levels while adding some extra activity to your day.
The Lesson
The point I'm trying to stress is that you shouldn't let a vacation be the excuse you're looking for to fall off your health journey. The main issue isn't the 5 days you're away, it's what comes after that.

The vacation itself isn't going to make or break your health. Five days of different food and a different routine are not what determine your long-term health. The bigger concern is whether those five days turn into five weeks of abandoning the habits you had before the vacation.

If you haven't been to the gym in a week because you were on vacation, it can be harder to get back into your routine. You're also used to eating fried fatty foods for meals, and desserts every night.

For many people, starting nutritious habits is much harder than maintaining an established routine. Skipping once is an exception, but twice is the start of a new trend. Keeping up on your health journey at all times is the best way to ensure you don't fall off it.

When you get back, the gym is normal, since you've been going the whole time anyway. You don't have to will yourself back into working out, because you never stopped in the first place.

Likewise, get back and cook a healthy dinner, and snack on some fruit for dessert. You got the cake out of your system, but you're not overly craving it because you didn't eat it all day every day.
Conclusion
There you have it. You can have a vacation without going completely overboard on indulging, and you can also have a vacation without obsessively restricting yourself. The goal isn't to eat perfectly. It's to find a balance that lets you enjoy your vacation while still taking care of yourself.

My approach might not be your approach, and that's okay. Maybe you want to hit the gym every morning like I do. Maybe you'd rather go for a long walk and call it a day. Maybe you want to eat a giant slice of cake every night. Maybe you don't care about any of this and want to eat cake for breakfast, lunch, and dinner. More power to you.

The important thing is to make informed decisions. Enjoy the food. Try something new. Have the dessert. Get some exercise. Eat some vegetables. Walk around and explore. And when you get home, get back to your normal routine.

You don't have to choose between being healthy and enjoying your vacation. You can do both.
