Description: |
  Did you know that German Chocolate Cake isn't German?  It was named after American baker Samuel German, who created Baker's German Sweet Chocolate and the Baker's Chocolate Company in 1852. In 1957, Lucy Clay then submitted a cake recipe in the Dallas Morning News, titled "German's Chocolate Cake". The apostrophe was eventually dropped, and German Chocolate Cake was born.<br><br>

  A traditional German chocolate cake is a chocolate cake with a chocolate frosting, and a filling of coconut and pecans. Whole delicious, it is filled with not so healthful ingredients, such as white and brown sugar, butter and oil, refined flour, and sweetener coconut.<br><br>

  On the other hand, this cake here is gluten free, vegan, and sugar free. There's no refined flour, added fats, or added sugars. Instead, it gets plenty of healthy fats from almond flour and chopped pecans. It gets whole food carbohydrates from oat flour without the need for white flour, and the sugar free syrup provides sweetness for significantly less calories, without any added sugar.<br><br>

  This healthy, whole foods cake uses no butter, eggs, or milk, making it vegan, while still being creamy, moist and delicious. No one would know that you're serving them a healthy vegan and gluten free cake full of whole food ingredients instead of refined junk.
Instructions:
- Preheat your oven to 350F. Line 2 9" cake pans with parchment paper, and lightly grease them with oil.

- Whisk together the dry ingredients in a large bowl - oat flour, almond flour, cocoa powder, baking powder, baking soda, and salt.

- In a separate large bowl, whisk together the wet ingredients - applesauce, sugar free syrup, almond milk, apple cider vinegar, and vanilla extract.

- Add the dry ingredients into the wet, and mix until just fully combined with a silicone spatula.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Healthier Dessert/German/bowl.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Evenly divide the cake batter across the 2 pans, about 720 g or 3 cups into each pan.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Healthier Dessert/German/raw.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Bake the cakes at 350F for about 40 minutes. The cakes should be slightly firm in the center, where a toothpick to the middle comes out nearly clean, and the internal temperature is around 200F. Let the cakes cool in the pan for 10 minutes before transferring to a wire rack. Allow them to cool completely, at least 1 hour in the fridge.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Healthier Dessert/German/baked.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Add almond butter, cocoa powder, vanilla extract, and sugar free syrup to a medium bowl. Mix until fully combined with a silicone spatula. Set your frosting aside.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Healthier Dessert/German/frosting.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Add chopped pecans and coconut flakes to a dry pan over medium heat. Toast for about 3 minutes, or until fragrant and lightly browned.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Healthier Dessert/German/toasted.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Combine the toasted pecans and coconut flakes with vanilla extract and sugar free syrup in a medium bowl. Set the filling aside.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Healthier Dessert/German/filling.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Place your first cake layer upside down on the plate, so the flatter side faces up.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Healthier Dessert/German/layer1.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Spread about 2/3 of your filling mixture (~230 g or 1 cup) onto the bottom (now top) of the first layer.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Healthier Dessert/German/filled.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Add the second layer on top, with the "bottom" (flatter side) facing up.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Healthier Dessert/German/layer2.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Carefully frost the top and sides of the cake. Add your remaining filling mixture (~115 g or 1/2 cup) on top of the frosting on top of the cake. Optionally garnish with more coconut flakes.
- <img src='/assets/Food/Healthier Dessert/German/topped.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>
Notes:
- I only baked a half batch at first to test the recipe, which is why the photos in the construction of the cake are of half a cake.

- Pecans are traditional in German Chocolate Cake, but nothing about this cake is traditional.  So go for walnuts or something else if that's all you have.

- I'm using sugar free syrup to make this cake sugar-free, but you can also use honey, maple syrup, or agave.  Note that since this cake contains a significant amount of sweetener, using honey, maple syrup, or agave will add a significant amount of calories and added sugar.

- |
  Please note though, that whole this cake contains healthy ingredients, it is not a low calorie dessert. Each of the 16 slices contains roughly 250 calories. That's roughly half the calories per slice as a <a href='https://tastesbetterfromscratch.com/german-chocolate-cake/#wprm-recipe-container-10369'>traditinal German Chocolate Cake</a>.<br><br>

  While the calories are lower, this is still relatively high-calorie.  The difference here is that the calories come from much better sources. Instead of being filled with saturated fat, empty starch, and added sugar, my cake here has more fiber, protein, vitamins, minerals, and antioxidants without the refined sugar, flour, or oil.  Something can be healthy and high calorie (this cake), or unhealthy and low calorie (ex: jelly beans).<br><br>

  So while you should enjoy this cake, please consume it in moderation, since it is still quite rich in fat, total sugar, and calories, especially if you opt for honey (or maple syrup or agave) in place of the sugar free syrup, which adds roughly 100 calories per slice.
