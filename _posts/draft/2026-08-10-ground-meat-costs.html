But many people but these fattier varieties because they're cheaper. But are they actually cheaper?

At first glance, yes fattier cuts of meat are cheaper. Let's look at beef prices in my grocery store. Per pound, the prices are the following:

With fattier ground meat, you're paying less, but you're also getting less. That's because proportionally more of the weight is made of up fat, so you get comparatively less muscle.

Let's assume that 93% lean meat it exactly 93% meat and 7% fat. And let's also make the same respective assumptions for 85% lean and 80% lean beef. If you were to drain away all the fat, what is the net cost of meat per pound?
{% assign net_80 = gross_80 | divided_by: 0.80 | round: 2 %} {% assign net_85 = gross_85 | divided_by: 0.85 | round: 2 %} {% assign net_93 = gross_93 | divided_by: 0.93 | round: 2 %}
  • 80%: ${{gross_80}} ÷ 0.80 = ${{net_80}}
  • 85%: ${{gross_85}} ÷ 0.85 = ${{net_85}}
  • 93%: ${{gross_93}} ÷ 0.93 = ${{net_93}}

So while, yes, leaner ground meat is more expensive per pound than fattier meat, the difference is less stark as it may seem when just looking at the gross prices.

Finally, let's compare the cost of the meats by its most important attribute: protein. For many people, the primary reason for eating meat is its protein content. For the cost, you want to maximize the amount of protein while minimizing the cost of non-protein calories.
