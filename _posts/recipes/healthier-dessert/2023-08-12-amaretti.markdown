Description: Amaretti <a href='/cookies'>cookies</a> are an Italian classic at any holiday, and always my favorite <a href='/recipes/healthier-dessert'>dessert</a> at the table.  Traditionally containing <a href='/misc/nuts#almonds'>almond flour</a> instead of <a href='/misc/grains#white-wheat'>white flour</a> and no <a href='/misc/dairy#butter'>butter</a> or oil, amaretti <a href='/cookies'>cookies</a> can be made healthy with very little changes.  This recipe is identical to the traditional, except with granulated <a href='/misc/hidden-sugar'>sugar</a> swapped for <a href='https://amzn.to/4ceFnvP'>granulated monk fruit</a>.  Or you can make my <a href='/recipes/amaretti-cookies-with-dates'>Amaretti Cookies with Dates</a> instead if you prefer a more naturally sweetened version.  Both are <a href='/misc/celiac'>gluten free</a>, don't have any <a href='/misc/dairy#butter'>butter</a> or oil, and get their <a href='/misc/fats'>healthy fats</a> from <a href='/misc/nuts#almonds'>almonds</a>.  Or check out any of my other <a href='/cookies'>cookie recipes</a>!  To use up those leftover <a href='/misc/meat#eggs'>yolks</a>, whip up a quick <a href='/recipes/hollandaise'>Greek Yogurt Hollandaise Sauce</a>.
Instructions:
- In a <a href='https://amzn.to/4adxMft'>large bowl</a>, mix together the <a href='/misc/nuts#almonds'>almond flour</a>, <a href='https://amzn.to/4ceFnvP'>granulated sweetener</a>, and salt

- In a separate bowl (glass or metal), beat the <a href='/misc/meat#egg-whites'>egg whites</a> on high speed until medium to stiff peaks. Fresh <a href='/misc/meat#egg-whites'>egg whites</a> are recommended here, as <a href='/misc/meat#egg-whites'>carton egg whites</a> will not beat up as well.  A plastic bowl will also prevent the <a href='/misc/meat#egg-whites'>egg whites</a> from whipping properly

- Add the extracts to the <a href='/misc/meat#eggs'>eggs</a> and beat to mix

- Gently fold the <a href='/misc/meat#eggs'>egg</a> mixture into the flour mixture with a <a href='https://amzn.to/43FEbwe'>silicone spatula</a>. Mix gently until just combined. Chill dough in the fridge for an hour

- Preheat the oven to 325F and line 2 <a href='https://amzn.to/45sRAsB'>cookie sheets</a> with parchment paper.  In a <a href='https://amzn.to/4adxMft'>small bowl</a>, add the <a href='https://amzn.to/43vQs5E'>powdered sweetener</a>

- Scoop your dough into 36 cookie dough balls, about 16 g each.  Roll each cookie into a small ball, roll the cookies in the <a href='https://amzn.to/43vQs5E'>powdered sweetener</a>, transfer to the <a href='https://amzn.to/45sRAsB'>cookie sheet</a>, and lightly flatten with your fingers

- Bake for 12-13 minutes, rotating the sheets halfway through, or until lighly browned on the sides. Let cool on pan for 5 minutes, or until you can safely lift them up.  Transfer to a <a href='https://amzn.to/4qQNmn2'>cooling rack</a>

Notes:
- Below is a table comparing if you used either <a href='https://amzn.to/4ceFnvP'>monk fruit</a> or <a href='/misc/hidden-sugar'>sugar</a> as the sweetener.  For the former, that means <b>1 cup (200 g) <a href='https://amzn.to/4ceFnvP'>granulated monk fruit</a></b> in the cookies, and rolling the dough in <b>3 tbsp (24 g) powdered monk fruit</b>.  The latter involes using <b>1 cup (200 g) granulated <a href='/misc/hidden-sugar'>sugar</a></b> instead in the cookies, and <b>3 tbsp (24 g) powdered sugar</b> for the rolling
