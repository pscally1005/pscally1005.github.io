Description: Gnocchi is a type of Italian <a href='/misc/grains#pasta'>pasta</a> dumplings that's normally made by combining cooked <a href='/misc/veggies#potato'>potatoes</a> with flour and <a href='/misc/meat#eggs'>egg</a> until you get a pillow-y dough.  Lesser known though is <a href='/misc/homemade-cheese#ricotta'>ricotta</a> gnocchi, which replaces the <a href='/misc/veggies#potato'>potatoes</a> with <a href='/misc/homemade-cheese#ricotta'>ricotta cheese</a>, shortening the cooking time and leading to a fluffy gnocchi.  What's definitely not known is <a href='/misc/dairy#yogurt'>Greek yogurt</a> gnocchi, which I'm just making up now.  By straining <a href='/misc/dairy#yogurt'>Greek yogurt</a> to use as our gnocchi base, we're essentially making <a href='/misc/homemade-cheese#labneh'>labneh cheese</a> for a <a href='/misc/high-protein'>high protein</a>, lower <a href='/misc/carbs'>carb</a> gnocchi.  Additionally, I'm swapping the <a href='/misc/grains#white-wheat'>refined white flour</a> for <a href='/misc/grains#whole-wheat'>whole wheat flour</a> for additional <a href='/misc/fiber'>fiber</a>, <a href='/misc/nutrient-alphabet'>vitamins, and minerals</a>.
Instructions:
- Place a <a href='https://amzn.to/4q2FwWu'>fine mesh strainer</a> over a <a href='https://amzn.to/4adxMft'>medium bowl</a>.  Add the <a href='/misc/dairy#yogurt'>yogurt</a> to the strainer, and mix in salt with a <a href='https://amzn.to/40F2aK0'>silicone spatula</a>. Place the strainer over the bowl into the fridge, and allow it to drain (uncovered) for about 24 hours
- <img src='/assets/Misc/Experiments/Cheese/labneh-separated.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Add the strained <a href='/misc/dairy#yogurt'>yogurt</a> to a <a href='https://amzn.to/4adxMft'>large bowl</a>.  Beat in an <a href='/misc/meat#eggs'>egg</a>, and mix in the <a href='/misc/dairy#grated-cheese'>grated cheese</a> with a <a href='https://amzn.to/43FEbwe'>silicone spatula</a>, making sure everything is fully combined without lumps before adding the flour

- Add in the flour, and mix with a <a href='https://amzn.to/43FEbwe'>silicone spatula</a> until you can't incorporate any more with the <a href='https://amzn.to/43FEbwe'>spatula</a>, and then finish kneading together with your hands. You should have a slightly sticky dough with the flour fully incorporated
- <img src='/assets/Food/Sides/Yogurt Gnocchi/bowl.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Turn the dough onto a clean work surface.  In batches, roll into a long and thin rope, and cut into 1" sized pieces
- <img src='/assets/Food/Sides/Yogurt Gnocchi/cut.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>

- Bring a pot of salted water to a boil.  Place in the gnocchi, and boil for about 3 minutes, or until they float to the top.  Remove with a <a href='https://amzn.to/43zKQHD'>spider</a>, and serve with any sauce you desire
- <img src='/assets/Food/Sides/Yogurt Gnocchi/boil.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>
- <img src='/assets/Food/Sides/Yogurt Gnocchi/plate.jpg' alt='' class='instruction-image'>
Notes:
- I ended up with about 715 g of strained <a href='/misc/dairy#yogurt'>yogurt</a>

- Optionally, roll each gnocchi on the back of a fork to make the ridges

- To turn this into a meal, I mixed the full batch of gnocchi with a full batch of <a href='/recipes/pasta-sauce'>Simple Pasta Sauce</a>, as well as 4 pounds of frozen <a href='/misc/veggies#broccoli'>broccoli</a> that I <a href='/recipes/roasted-vegetables'>roasted</a>, as well as some <a href='/misc/dairy#grated-cheese'>parmesan</a> to make myself lunches for the next 8 days
