Description: <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>Butternut squash</a> was my gateway <a href='/misc/veggies'>vegetable</a>. I love most <a href='/misc/veggies'>veggies</a> now, but growing up, I hated almost all of them. <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>Butternut squash</a> was the one that was able to unlock my love of <a href='/misc/veggies'>vegetables</a>, and this recipe how my mom would prepare them. Butternut is a <a href='/misc/carbs'>starchy</a> <a href='/misc/veggies'>vegetable</a>, similar to <a href='/misc/veggies#potato'>potatoes</a> or <a href='/misc/veggies#plantain'>plantains</a>, so I tend to eat this more as my <a href='/misc/carbs'>carb</a> <a href='/recipes/sides'>side</a> than a <a href='/misc/veggies'>vegetable</a> <a href='/recipes/sides'>side</a>, such as pairing this with <a href='/recipes/grilled-shrimp'>Grilled Shrimp Skewers</a> and <a href='/recipes/vegetable-medley'>Spinach and Onion Vegetable Medley</a> for a delicious and well rounded meal.
Instructions:
- Preheat your oven to 400F, and line a large <a href='https://amzn.to/45sRAsB'>cookie sheet</a> with parchment paper

- Cut your <a href='/misc/veggies#onion'>onion</a> into a large dice, and add to the pan. Add the <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a>, <a href='/misc/olive-oil'>oil</a>, <a href='/misc/veggies#garlic'>minced garlic</a>, garlic powder, onion powder, salt, and black pepper

- Roast at 400F for about 30 minutes, or until soft and browned

Notes:
- Today I'm going with frozen <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a>, but fresh will also work. Just scale the recipe up based on how many pounds your <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a> is. Typically 1 large <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a> is about 3 pounds, so triple all the seasonings

- |
  As for the cost between frozen or fresh <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a>, I got this 1 lb (454 g) bag of frozen <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a> for $1.99, already peeled and cubed.<br><br>

  In comparison, I can get a fresh butternut for $0.79/lb, but that includes the weight of the skins and seeds, plus the work to cut it up myself.  This 2.76 lb (1,257 g) <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a> cost $2.19, and yielded 2.42 lb (1,100 g) of raw <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a> meat.  If you don't peel the <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>squash</a> (lately I haven't been, the skin is completely edible), then the weight of the raw <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a> is 2.68 lb (1,215 g).<br><br>

  Therefore, the net cost of fresh <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a> is $0.90/lb for just the raw squash meat, or $0.82/lb if you include the skins.  Most grocery stores also tend to sell fresh <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a> already peeled and cubed.  At my store, that would cost about $2.99/lb.<br><br>

  This makes buying a fresh <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a> less than half the price of the frozen, and less than a third of the price of the fresh pre-cut <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>squash</a>.  It's up for you to decide if that cost difference is work the extra effort.  Peeling is the most time intensive part of preparing <a href='/misc/veggies#butternut-squash'>butternut squash</a>, and you can easily just skip that and eat the skins, which is less work and more nutritious.  Win-win!
