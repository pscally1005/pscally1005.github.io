Description: Yesterday I made <a href="/recipes/yellow-rice">Anti-Inflammatory Yellow(ish) Rice</a>, but today I'm making a simple, non-traditional risotto using healthier brown rice instead of white arborio rice. Brown rice has more <a href="/misc/protein">protein</a>, <a href="/misc/fiber">fiber</a>, and micronutrients, like vitamins <a href="/misc/nutrient-alphabet#T">B1</a>, <a href="/misc/nutrient-alphabet#R">B2</a>, <a href="/misc/nutrient-alphabet#N">B3</a>, B6, <a href="/misc/nutrient-alphabet#E">E</a>, and <a href="/misc/nutrient-alphabet#K">K</a>, as well as copper, <a href="/misc/nutrient-alphabet#M">magnesium</a>, manganese, phosphorus, <a href="/misc/nutrient-alphabet#P">potassium</a>, <a href="/misc/nutrient-alphabet#S">selenium</a>, and <a href="/misc/nutrient-alphabet#Z">zinc</a>. This is a simple base side dish that can have whatever spices you want mixed into it. Or mix in some meat and vegetables to make it a complete meal. Butternut squash is great in risotto, as it melts right into it, making it creamier without the need for butter. Check out my <a href="/recipes/butternut-squash-risotto">Butternut Squash RisOATto</a> for a risotto recipe using rolled oats!
Instructions: 
- In a medium pan over medium heat, add rice and toast until fragrant, about 3-5 minutes

- Add in a little bit of your broth and stir. Cook until most of the water has evaporated. Repeat until you have no broth left and the rice is al dente, about 30 minutes. You don't need to stir the rice constantly, only every minute or so

- Stir in grated cheese and lemon juice until creamy. Add in any spices you desire
