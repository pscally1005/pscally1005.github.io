Description: As someone who cooks with minimal oil, I need to make sure to be working healthy <a href='/misc/fats'>fats</a> into my diet. Today, I'm mixing together whole milk <a href='/misc/dairy#yogurt'>Greek yogurt</a>, <a href='/misc/seeds#flax-seeds'>flaxseed</a>, and <a href='/recipes/natural-peanut-butter'>nut butter</a>. This yogurt bowl is a great high <a href='/misc/protein'>protein</a>, <a href='/misc/fats'>fat</a>, and <a href='/misc/fiber'>fiber</a> breakfast. Because of the full <a href='/misc/fats'>fat</a> dairy, this breakfast is a good source of the newly discovered <a href='https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10649853/'>essential fatty acid C15</a>, which has been shown to be one of the biggest contributors for longevity science. It's relatively low <a href='/misc/carbs'>carb</a>, has no <a href='/misc/hidden-sugar'>added sugar</a>, and is perfectly sweet from the monk fruit and berries. It also has no protein powder if you avoid that, but still packs 34 g of <a href='/misc/protein'>protein</a> per serving.  You can also check out any of my other <a href='/yogurt'>yogurt recipes</a>!
Instructions:
- Mix all ingredients together in a small bowl, and top with blueberries
Notes:

    <li>Top with any other berries, like strawberries, blackberries, or raspberries</li>
    <li>For the same <a href='/misc/calories'>calories</a> and <a href='/misc/fats'>fat</a>, you can also use an equal amount (3/4 cup, 170 g) plain nonfat greek yogurt, and add 1/2 tbsp (7 g) extra virgin coconut oil (or olvie oil)</li>
  </ul>
