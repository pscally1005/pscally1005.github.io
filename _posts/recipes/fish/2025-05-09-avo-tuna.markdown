Description: This healthy <a href='/misc/fish#tuna'>tuna</a> salad, or <a href='/misc/fish#tuna'>tuna</a> fish, uses a base of <a href='/misc/fruit#avocado'>avocado</a> instead of mayonnaise. That means it's significantly lower in <a href='/misc/fats'>fat</a> and <a href='/misc/calories'>calories</a>, and the quality of <a href='/misc/fats'>fat</a> is much higher. Avocados are high in heart healthy mono unsaturated <a href='/misc/fats'>fats</a>, as opposed to mayo, which is nothing but empty <a href='/misc/calories'>calorie</a> inflammatory seed oils. With <a href='/misc/fish#tuna'>tuna</a> canned in water, this high <a href='/misc/protein'>protein</a> dish can be used as a sandwich filling, salad topper, or even a dip for raw <a href='/misc/veggies'>vegetables</a>.  For a <a href='/misc/dairy#yogurt'>Greek yogurt</a> based <a href='/misc/fish#tuna'>tuna</a> salad, check out my <a href='/recipes/tuna-salad'>No-Mayo Tuna Salad</a>
Instructions:
- Cut your onion into a fine dice, and add to a medium bowl. Add the avocado to the bowl, and mash with the back of a fork. Drain your tuna, and add to the bowl with the rest of the ingredients. Mix until fully combined
